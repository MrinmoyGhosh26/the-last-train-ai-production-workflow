# Shot Design — The Last Train

The story was divided into six short shots so that each
generation had a clear visual and narrative purpose.

The shots were generated separately rather than asking the
model to create the entire sequence at once.

## Shot 1 — Establishing

**Purpose:** Establish the protagonist, location and mood.

The character stands alone at an almost-empty metro station
late at night.

**Camera:** Slow cinematic push toward the character.

**Key requirements:**
- Same character reference
- Nighttime metro environment
- Photorealistic look
- Vertical 9:16 framing
- Minimal movement

---

## Shot 2 — Phone

**Purpose:** Introduce the event that starts the story.

The character notices his phone vibrating, takes it out and
looks at the screen.

**Key requirements:**
- Maintain character appearance
- Controlled hand movement
- Phone remains consistent
- Keep the camera vertical

**Generation lesson:** Hand and phone interactions were one
of the areas where the model was least reliable, so the action
was kept as simple as possible.

---

## Shot 3 — The Warning

**Purpose:** Reveal the first mysterious message.

The character looks at his phone and reacts with confusion
before looking up.

The exact notification text was added during post-production:

> **DON'T LOOK BACK.**

**Reason for post-production text:** AI-generated text was not
reliable enough to guarantee accurate typography.

---

## Shot 4 — Looking Around

**Purpose:** Transition from the phone to the mystery.

The character looks around the metro platform as if he has
noticed something unusual.

**Camera:** Controlled cinematic movement.

**Key requirements:**
- Same environment
- Same character
- Natural facial reaction
- No unnecessary movement
- Vertical 9:16 framing

---

## Shot 5 — The Double

**Purpose:** Reveal the central mystery.

The character turns and sees a mysterious man several meters
away.

The mysterious man looks exactly like the protagonist.

**Key requirements:**
- Matching face
- Matching hairstyle
- Matching clothing
- Same general build
- Both characters remain still
- No unnecessary movement

The scene was intentionally simplified after more complicated
platform positioning produced unreliable results.

---

## Shot 6 — The Last Train

**Purpose:** End the sequence on an unresolved visual moment.

The protagonist and his double continue staring at each other
while a metro train passes along the side of the platform.

The video ends while the train is still passing.

The final message is added during post-production:

> **I TOLD YOU NOT TO LOOK.**

The video then cuts to black.

---

## Shot Design Principles

The main principle throughout generation was to keep each shot
focused on one clear action.

When a shot required too many simultaneous actions or complex
spatial relationships, the scene was simplified.

This helped make the generation process more predictable and
made individual failures easier to identify and correct.****
